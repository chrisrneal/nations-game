# The 100x plan

2026-09-30. Written by an autonomous build session that the owner asked to "make
it roughly 100x better, not 10% better", with full authority to merge (decision
record H1). This file is the diagnosis and the plan; docs/PROGRESS.md logs what
was actually merged.

## 1. What the game is today

A mobile PWA. You pick one of 17 real nations in 2030; 16 others and 6 regions
are run by a deterministic utility AI. A month passes every 10 seconds at 1x
(or every 30 minutes on the live clock). Each month the inbox shows cards:
"Food short by 82 next month", "Energy short by 537 next month", an occasional
crisis appeal, offers from AI nations. You tap the pre-filled answer. After 60
months you get a score: your output against your own IMF baseline, times a
world multiplier.

**Core loop today:** read shortfall card → tap "buy from X at world price" →
wait → repeat. Twice a year, pay a crisis share that your standing policy has
usually paid already.

The engine under it is excellent: pure, deterministic across Node and Chromium,
1,000-seed hash tests, saves with migrations, an explainable AI, a balance
harness, offline PWA, 60 fps at 4x on a slowed phone. None of that is the
problem.

## 2. Structural diagnosis (blunt)

1. **Every decision has one right answer.** The shortfall card's first option is
   a fair offer; the alternatives only lose. 72% of crisis appeals are prepaid by
   the default dial before the player sees them. The go/no-go review measured
   this; I reproduced it by playing: Japan gets the same two cards, every month,
   for five years.
2. **Money means nothing.** A month of Japan's food imports costs 9 Credit out of
   671 held. By month 60 every nation holds about 50 months of output in unspent
   Credit. Sinks are 0.7% of income. Nothing competes for Credit, so nothing the
   player pays for is a sacrifice.
3. **Collaboration has one verb, and it is a chore.** "Collaboration over
   conquest" is expressed only as bilateral fair trades and one pool per crisis
   type. There is nothing to build together, nothing to risk on a partner,
   nothing a partner can let you down on except a trade the sim settles
   instantly.
4. **A third of the roster has no lever.** Japan, Korea and Turkiye sit at the
   30% shortfall-penalty cap. Below the cap, the next unit of food or energy is
   worth zero output. Two attempts (prompts 17, 17b) to give them a lever through
   home investment failed on exactly this dead zone.
5. **The AI nations react; they never initiate anything collaborative.** They
   answer offers, send offers and pay into pools. They never propose a shared
   plan, never recruit, never walk out of one.
6. **No arc and no feedback during play.** "100% of baseline" is the only
   progress signal. Rank, the world multiplier, what is coming next month and
   whether the world is winning are all invisible until the end screen.
7. **The process premise is deadlocked.** ROADMAP says no new collaboration
   system before Gate 2 passes. Gate 2 needs 10 playtests where "most want
   another game", of a game whose decisions have obvious answers. Seventeen
   prompts went into balance lines graded on seeds no player ever sees; zero
   playtests exist. The gate cannot pass until the game is worth playing, and
   the gate forbids making it worth playing.

## 3. The 100x thesis

The engine is done; the game on top of it is a chore loop. The 100x is to make
each 2-5 minute check-in a handful of real bets with other nations: **build
things together** that the world is short of, with money that is finally scarce
early and partners who can walk out, against crises that can wreck what you
built unless the pools are funded. Three dimensions:

1. **Collaboration depth: joint projects** (RULES 13). Hosted consortia (solar
   belts, hydrogen corridors, grain corridors, desalination, early-warning
   networks, vaccine plants) that create the food and energy the world lacks.
   Surplus nations host, deficit nations finance, shares follow what each paid,
   leaving mid-build is possible and remembered, climate damage at the host cuts
   the yield. Collaboration becomes the route to self-reliance instead of an
   alternative to it, and Credit gets a use worth sacrificing for.
2. **AI nations with agency.** They found consortia, invite the partners they
   trust and need, pitch the player with numbers, and walk out under stress.
3. **Session design for the phone.** A supply autopilot removes the monthly
   chore cards; the home screen becomes a briefing (rank, world accord, what
   comes next); the end screen becomes a debrief with a reason to play again.

Plus one structural fix these need: **no dead zone in the shortfall penalty**
(decision H3), so every unit of food or energy moves output for every nation.

**Deliberately not optimized:** multiplayer and networking, war, the
top-scorer fairness statistic and the 200-seed archetype lines (reported, not
tuned for: H2), visual art, data re-sourcing, performance (already inside every
budget).

## 4. Premises replaced

- **"No new collaboration system before Gate 2 passes."** Replaced by H2: a new
  system merges when it keeps the invariants (determinism, conservation, no
  negative stocks, sabotage never pays, trading beats isolating) and creates real
  decisions; fairness lines are reported, not blocking.
- **"Credit competes with imports."** It cannot: imports are priced at about 1%
  of income. Joint projects are the competition.
- **"The shortfall penalty caps at 30%."** The cap made the goods a deep importer
  gets worth nothing. H3 replaces it with a flatter line and no reachable cap.
- **"Shared threshold" was invisible.** The vision says a shared threshold plus an
  individual ranking. The multiplier is continuous and stays so (D3); a visible
  World Accord threshold says whether the world made it, on top of the ranking.

## 7. Still open, and the next three slices

Open, in docs/GAPS.md: nobody has played it yet (the playtest kit is ready); the
hydrogen corridor is built in 52% of games (Gate 3's line is 50%); the vaccine network
is never built; Japan is strong in all-AI worlds after projects; the project sink is
small, so Credit is still abundant after the first year.

1. **Run the playtests** (owner): 10 games, 3+ by others, with docs/playtests/README.md.
   Nothing else answers whether it is fun.
2. **A first-game coach**: three cards in the first months that point at the briefing,
   an invitation and the Projects tab; today a new player meets four tabs cold.
3. **Credit that binds**: give Credit a monthly claim that grows with ambition (upkeep
   on running projects, or a budget share of output), so that a year-3 nation chooses
   between a project, the pools and resilience instead of affording all of them.
